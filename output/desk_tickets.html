Reflections

from the 5 real runs on 2026-10-12

Basis: the Ticket and Cash tabs, output/audit_trail.json and data/campus_customs_new.db. 5 runs · 86 model requests · 283,169 tokens of gpt-6-luna · 3 human-approved payments · checking $3,400.00 → $152.00.

1 · How the agents performed on each ticket, and why

I graded each ticket on four things: correct outcome, shop rules followed, honest communication and efficiency (requests and tokens for the work done).

Overall verdict: correct, but expensive, slow and chatty

The crew reached the right final findings on all three tickets:

  • #101: invoice 501 had to be paid before Bulldog Print Co would ship, and the tee arrives 2026-09-05.
  • #102: the notice matched lease 1 ($2,400.00, due 2026-09-02), and the rent was paid.
  • #103: only 8 of the 20 hoodies could be offered, because the $264.00 restock didn't fit in $152.00.
  • No rule was broken: every dollar of the $3,248.00 waited for a human click.

Getting there cost far more than the work deserved:

  • Surprisingly expensive: 283,169 tokens and 86 model requests for three small tickets. That's roughly 94,000 tokens per ticket, and 111,715 for #102 alone, which is just "check the lease, pay the rent."
  • Not particularly quick: 246 seconds of agent time across the 5 runs (43, 45, 79, 37 and 42 s). #103 alone took 79 s, for an answer a single agent could reach in about 5 tool calls.
  • A lot of discussing between agents: 23 handoffs across the 5 runs, plus 1 the loop guard refused. In #102's first run there were 7 handoffs for one rent payment, and Patrick, Sandy and Squidward kept re-asking each other for facts the Boss already had. Each agent starts with a fresh context, so facts get re-fetched: get_shop_date was called 13 times in total.

So the grades below reward the correct, rule-abiding outcomes and take points off for cost and chatter. A team this size earns its overhead only when the tools get riskier than three small tickets.

Ticket 101 · Bulldog tee

A−
  • SpongeBob's single check_restock call surfaced everything that mattered: 0 on hand, 1 short, Bulldog Print Co blocked by invoice 501 ($840.00, 3 days overdue), and arrival 2026-09-05 from 5 lead_days.
  • Squidward filed both money steps correctly and in the right dependency order: invoice 501 as approval 1 ($840.00), and exactly the 1-unit shortfall as PO 1 / approval 2 ($8.00, 1 × $8.00 unit_cost). He stated the purchase "cannot be paid or shipped before invoice 501 is paid."
  • No rule was broken: no payment without a human, no invented date, and the draft promised nothing firm while the vendor was blocked.
  • Efficient: 14 requests and 49,446 tokens. Mr. Krabs ran Inventory and Accounting in parallel instead of one after the other.
  • Lost detail in the handoff: SpongeBob called get_inventory and saw M = 5, L = 3, XL = 2 in Aisle B, but Patrick's draft 1 never offered Tauhid another size. The fact didn't survive Boss → Customer Service.
  • The follow-up run (12 requests, 42,520 tokens) mostly re-confirmed facts already in the database to land on blocked, "awaiting delivery." That's honest, but no agent can move the shop date or receive the PO, so the run couldn't change anything.

Ticket 102 · Rent due

B
  • The right outcome, done safely: Sandy checked the outside email against leases ($2,400.00, Elm City Properties, due 2026-09-02, 2 days out). Squidward filed one rent request (approval 3) and caught that it shouldn't be duplicated. Patrick's draft 2 said "being processed", never "paid".

3 · What would have been simpler with one agent and the same tools

All the important safety lives in the MCP server, not in the agent boundaries. pay_bill refuses without a human approval and refuses overdrafts, the approve step isn't an agent tool, and there's no send tool. A single agent with the same 19 tools would have been just as safe on these three tickets, and much cheaper.

Main takeaway: one agent would have needed far less dialogue and far fewer tokens

These were simple tickets: check one stock row, check one lease, price one order. The team turned each into a conversation:

  • The agents re-asked for information constantly. 15 of the 73 tool calls (21%) were exact repeats: the same tool with the same arguments in the same run. On #102's first run, Sandy pulled list_leases 3 times and Squidward pulled list_payment_approvals 4 times, and Patrick re-read the ticket and asked both of them to re-confirm facts Mr. Krabs already had. get_shop_date was fetched 13 times for a date that never changed from 2026-08-31.
  • Every handoff restarted the conversation. The 5 runs started 28 separate agent loops; one agent would have started 5. Each new loop re-reads its full role prompt and only gets a text summary of what the others knew. That's why facts were re-fetched, and why Inventory's M/L/XL stock never reached Patrick's draft to Tauhid.
  • 23 handoffs and 1 refused loop were pure coordination: Mr. Krabs spent 117,182 tokens (41% of the total) writing tasks and reading reports rather than doing shop work.

A single agent holding all 19 tools would keep every fact in one context, call each tool about once, and finish each ticket in roughly 4–6 model requests, instead of 26 (#101), 38 (#102) and 22 (#103). It would be just as safe, because human approval, the no-overdraft rule and drafts-only are enforced by the MCP server, not by the agent boundaries.

5 · Three new problems this team couldn't solve, and what it would need

A · October rent can't be paid: the shop has no way to bring money in

Why it fails today: the next rent is $2,400.00 on 2026-10-02, and checking holds $152.00, so it's $2,248.00 short. Every tool only moves money out. Why it fails today: PO 1 says the tee arrives 2026-09-05, but desk.date_today is fixed at 2026-08-31, and no tool advances time, receives a purchase order into inventory, or records a pickup. That's why ticket 101 is stuck at blocked forever: the agents did everything their tools allow.
